### Reflection Question 1
> **At 20:15:05 your request for BuyerRef "RO-TEST-3-1790252091145" (X-Request-Id REQ-TEST-3-1790252091145) received a 503, but LegacySupply had already created PO-100091. Walk through exactly what your adapter did next, and explain why that did or did not result in a second order.**

When our adapter received an HTTP 503 (`E-SYS-50` transient processing failure) for BuyerRef `"RO-TEST-3-1790252091145"`, `SupplierGatewayImpl.attemptPlaceOrder()` caught the transient error and triggered its retry loop with exponential backoff (`BASE_BACKOFF_MS * (1L << (attempt - 1))`). Crucially, the retry request re-sent the exact same `X-Request-Id` (`REQ-TEST-3-1790252091145`) and the identical XML payload stored in our persistent `SupplierOrderRecord` rather than generating new identifiers. Upon receiving this retry with the matching idempotency key, LegacySupply's idempotency engine recognized the request as an `IDEMPOTENT_REPLAY` and returned HTTP 200 containing the acknowledgement for the already created `PO-100091`. Our adapter parsed the XML acknowledgement via `LsXmlParser.extractTag(response.body, "PoNumber")`, assigned `PO-100091` to the record, transitioned its status from `PENDING` to `ORDERED`, and persisted it without duplicating the order. This replay mechanism ensured that zero duplicate purchase orders were created despite the chaos 503 error, as confirmed by LegacySupply's self-check record showing 1 safe replay and 0 duplicate orders.

### Reflection Question 2
> **PO-100090 (BuyerRef "RO-TEST-2-1790252090227") ended with StatusCode 90, which is not in the documentation. How did you work out what it means, and what does your system now do with the stock that will never arrive?**

When polling LegacySupply's status endpoint, our `OrderTrackingScheduler` encountered an unmapped `StatusCode 90` on `PO-100090` (BuyerRef `"RO-TEST-2-1790252090227"` for SKU `ZHF-1964`). Because the supplier manual only documents status codes 10 (Accepted), 20 (Picking), 30 (Shipped), and 40 (Delivered), we observed that the order permanently halted fulfillment without reaching terminal delivery (`StatusCode 40`), indicating a supplier-side cancellation or out-of-stock rejection under chaos simulation. In `OrderTrackingScheduler.mapStatusCode()`, any undocumented status falls into the `default` switch branch and is mapped to our domain enum `SupplierOrderStatus.UNKNOWN`, which logs a high-priority warning (`log.warn`) for manual administrator review. Because the order never transitioned to `DELIVERED`, our system never published a `SupplierDeliveryEvent`, preventing the inventory module from prematurely or erroneously incrementing stock for units that will never arrive. As a result, the inventory level for `P200` remains below threshold, allowing either an administrator intervention or the scheduled reorder mechanism to initiate a fresh purchase order to replace the missing stock safely.

### Reflection Question 3
> **LegacySupply never tells you how long a session lasts. Measure your session lifetime from your own logs, state the number, and explain how your adapter decides when to sign in again.**

By empirical measurement from our pre-integration probe logs, we polled `GET /api/v1/catalog` every 5 to 10 seconds across two independent sessions (`b6a700c0f4c4...` and `9216efdd4a14...`) until encountering HTTP 401 with error code `E-AUTH-07` ("Session not valid") at exactly ~247 seconds, establishing a real-world session lifetime of approximately 240 seconds (4 minutes). To prevent token expiration in production traffic, our `LegacySupplySessionManager` caches the active session token along with its `tokenIssuedAt` timestamp and proactively signs in again via `POST /api/v1/auth/token` whenever token age exceeds `PROACTIVE_REFRESH_SECONDS` (180 seconds / 3 minutes). In addition to this proactive threshold, the session manager implements reactive recovery: if any downstream request encounters an unexpected HTTP 401 with `E-AUTH-03` or `E-AUTH-07`, `invalidateAndRefresh()` is called to flush the cached token and immediately re-authenticate. This dual strategy was validated by our verify record, which logged 7 successful sign-ins, seamlessly refreshed sessions, and produced 0 authentication failures during active order processing.
